## 🌟 Executive Summary

This project brings together customer, sales, product, and workforce data in one Excel-based BI solution. 

The analysis shows that the business performs well overall, but a large share of revenue comes from a limited set of markets, customer groups, and product categories. The United States is the dominant market, A-segment customers generate most of the revenue, and Bikes remain the core product category. In parallel, workforce data points to potential operational pressure in Quality Assurance and Inventory Management, where average sick leave is relatively high.

Taken together, these findings suggest that future growth should not rely solely on current top-performing areas. A more balanced revenue mix, a more efficient product portfolio, and stronger workforce stability would improve long-term resilience.

### 📊 Key Metric Highlights

- **Market Concentration:** The US market represents **60.91% of customers** and **66.65% of total revenue**.
- **Pareto Distribution:** **13.5% of SKUs** generate nearly **80% of gross profit**, while A-segment customers (26.95% of base) generate **80% of revenue**.
- **Product Dominance:** Bikes account for **81.59%** of total revenue.
- **Workforce Risk:** QA (59.7 hours) and Inventory (54.4 hours) show the highest absenteeism.

## 🎯 Business Problem

The company operates across different customer segments, product lines, and internal departments. However, the available reporting structure did not provide one clear view of overall performance. This made it harder to identify where revenue was concentrated, which areas were over-performing, and where hidden operational risks existed.

Without an integrated dashboard system, decision-making was more reactive than strategic.

### Objectives

This project was built to:

- combine sales, customer, product, and HR data into one analytical model
- identify concentration risks across markets, customers, and product categories
- evaluate product performance and monthly demand patterns
- measure workforce indicators such as salary distribution and sick leave cost
- present key insights in a dashboard format that is easy to explore and explain

---

## 🔍 Key Insights

### 1) Customer & Revenue Overview

This dashboard focuses on customer distribution, revenue concentration, and market dependency.

- The **United States** represents **66.65% of total revenue**, while accounting for **60.91% of customers**. This shows a strong dependence on one market.
- **California** alone contributes **12.64% of revenue** from **11.13% of customers**, making it the most important state in the portfolio.
- **A-segment customers** make up **26.95% of the customer base** but generate nearly **80% of total revenue**.
- In contrast, **C-segment customers** account for **51.67% of customers** while contributing only **5.01% of revenue**.
- After California, **Texas** and **Washington** stand out as the next strongest revenue contributors.

**Why it matters:**  
The company has a solid customer base, but revenue is concentrated in a relatively small share of markets and customer groups. This creates both opportunity and risk.

---

### 2) Sales & Product Group Performance

This dashboard looks at category contribution, SKU concentration, and overall portfolio efficiency.

- **Bikes** generate **81.59% of total revenue**, making them by far the most dominant product category.
- A small share of products drives most of the business. Around **13.5% of SKUs generate about 80% of total gross profit**.
- Product groups such as **Touring Bikes** and **Frames** appear to offer room for broader commercial development.
- At the same time, a large long-tail assortment contributes little gross profit while adding operational complexity.

**Why it matters:**  
The portfolio contains clear winners, but it is also broader than necessary. A more selective product mix could reduce complexity without hurting revenue.

---

### 3) Product Deep Dive

This section examines monthly trends and seasonality patterns inside the sales dashboard.

- Revenue builds through the middle of the year and reaches its **peak in August**.
- **January** is the weakest month.
- Toward the end of the year, revenue momentum shows a decline of about **13%**.

**Why it matters:**  
The business is not equally strong across all months. Seasonal planning for inventory, campaigns, and staffing would help smooth performance.

---

### 4) Workforce Overview

This dashboard focuses on workforce distribution, cost structure, and absenteeism patterns.

- **63.8% of employees** are in **Manufacturing**, which shows that operational capacity is highly concentrated.
- **R&D** has the highest hourly salary at **$35.93**, followed by **Executive** roles at **$27.00**.
- **Quality Assurance** and **Inventory Management** report the highest average sick leave hours: **59.7** and **54.4** respectively.
- The highest average sick leave cost appears in the **Executive** group at **$1,443.40** per employee.

**Why it matters:**  
The workforce is not only a cost center; it also affects continuity. High absenteeism in key operational roles can create disruptions beyond HR metrics alone.

## 🚀 Strategic Risks

- Heavy dependence on the **US market**
- Revenue concentration in **A-segment customers**
- Strong overreliance on the **Bikes** category
- A broad long-tail product portfolio with limited commercial value
- Operational pressure caused by sick leave in **QA** and **Inventory Management**

---

## 🎯 Recommended Actions

- Protect high-value customer relationships, especially in the most profitable segment.
- Reduce market concentration over time by strengthening sales efforts outside the main revenue base.
- Review low-performing SKUs and simplify the product portfolio where possible.
- Develop adjacent product groups with cross-sell or bundling strategies.
- Monitor absenteeism trends in operational departments and address possible workload issues early.

---

## 🧭 Final Takeaway

The company is generating strong revenue, but too much of that performance depends on a narrow set of drivers. The dashboards make it easier to see where that concentration exists and where future pressure may emerge.

From a business perspective, the main priority is not just growth, but more balanced growth. Reducing dependency on a few key markets, products, and customer groups would make performance more stable over time.

## 🛠 Tools & Technical Proficiency

- **Data Transformation:** Power Query (M Language) for ETL, data cleaning, and structural normalization.
- **Data Modeling:** Power Pivot using **DAX (Data Analysis Expressions)** to create complex measures and establish a Star Schema relationship model.
- **Advanced Analytics:** Implemented **ABC Product Segmentation**, **Pareto (80/20) Revenue Analysis**, and **Market Share Concentration** metrics to identify key business drivers.
- **Visualization:** PivotTables, PivotCharts, and advanced conditional formatting for executive-level reporting.
- **Automation:** VBA-driven interactions (Slicer Reset, Navigation) to enhance user experience (UI/UX).
- **Business Intelligence:** Strategic storytelling, identifying revenue concentration risks, and providing actionable operational recommendations.

---

## Technical Data Architecture (Data Modeling)
The project utilizes a Star Schema optimized for analytical querying. The data model ensures referential integrity and supports complex DAX calculations across multiple dimensions:

- **Fact Tables:** Orders (Header level), Order_Items (Granular level).
- **Dimension Tables:** Customer (Demographics), Products (Hierarchy), Staff (HR Performance), and Calendar (Time Intelligence).
- **Key Relationships:** 1:N (One-to-Many) relationships established to ensure filtering flows correctly from Dimensions to Fact tables, enabling accurate “Slicing and Dicing” of KPIs.
